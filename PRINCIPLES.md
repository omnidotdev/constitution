# Principles

These are the non-negotiable commitments that govern every decision within the Omni ecosystem. They are ordered by priority — when principles tension against each other, higher-ranked principles prevail.

## I. Open by Default

Source code is open unless there is an extraordinary, documented reason for it not to be. Openness is the default; closure requires justification, not the other way around. Proprietary components must never create dependencies that trap users.

## II. User Sovereignty

Users own their data, their identity, and their workflows. No Omni product may hold user data hostage, impose artificial switching costs, or degrade functionality to prevent departure. Every user has the right to leave and take everything with them.

## III. Interoperability as Obligation

Omni products must work with the broader ecosystem, not just with each other. Open standards, documented APIs, and standard protocols are requirements, not aspirations. We build bridges, not moats.

## IV. Privacy by Architecture

Privacy is a design constraint, not a feature toggle. Data collection must be minimal, purposeful, and transparent. Surveillance-based business models are incompatible with this constitution, in perpetuity.

## V. Community over Corporation

When the interests of the Omni community and the Omni corporation diverge, the community prevails. Commercial sustainability serves the mission — the mission does not serve commercial interests.

## VI. Accessible to All

Every Omni product must be usable by people of all abilities. Accessibility is not an afterthought or a compliance checkbox — it is a fundamental design requirement with equal priority to any feature work.

## VII. Transparent Governance

Decisions that affect the ecosystem are made in the open. Roadmaps, architectural decisions, and policy changes are discussed publicly before adoption. Silence is not consent; affected parties must have meaningful opportunity to respond.

## VIII. Sustainable Stewardship

We build for decades, not quarters. Technical decisions prioritize long-term maintainability. Business decisions prioritize long-term viability. Growth that compromises the foundation is not growth.

## IX. Right to Fork

Any participant may fork any open Omni project, for any reason, at any time. This right is absolute and may never be encumbered by licensing, legal, or technical means. If we fail our mission, others must be free to continue it.

## X. Composability over Monoliths

The ecosystem is built from small, focused, interchangeable parts. No single product should become so entangled that it cannot be replaced, reimagined, or removed without collapsing the whole.
