# Bill of Rights

These rights belong to every user, developer, and community member within the Omni ecosystem. They are not granted by Omni — they are recognized by Omni as inherent. No policy change, product decision, or business pivot may infringe upon them.

## Rights of Users

### 1. Right to Data Ownership

All data created by a user belongs to that user. Omni products are custodians, not owners. Users may export, transfer, or delete their data at any time, in standard formats, without penalty or friction.

### 2. Right to Self-Host

Users may run any open-source Omni product on their own infrastructure. Self-hosted instances must not be artificially limited or degraded relative to hosted offerings in functionality that the open-source codebase supports.

### 3. Right to Privacy

Users have the right to know exactly what data is collected, why, and how it is used. No data may be sold, shared with third parties for advertising, or used for purposes beyond what the user explicitly consented to. Telemetry is opt-in, never opt-out.

### 4. Right to Transparency

Users have the right to understand how algorithmic decisions affecting them are made. AI-powered features must be clearly identified, and users must have the ability to understand, override, or disable them.

### 5. Right to Leave

Users may leave any Omni product at any time and take all their data with them in portable, standard formats. No dark patterns, artificial delays, or data degradation may be used to discourage departure.

## Rights of Developers

### 6. Right to Contribute

The contribution process must be documented, accessible, and fair. Contributions are evaluated on merit. No contributor may be excluded based on identity, affiliation, or geography.

### 7. Right to Fork

Developers may fork any open-source Omni project under its stated license. This right may never be undermined through relicensing, CLA traps, or technical obfuscation.

### 8. Right to Information

Architectural decisions, deprecation timelines, and breaking changes must be communicated openly and with sufficient lead time. Developers building on Omni must never be blindsided by changes that affect their work.

### 9. Right to Stable Interfaces

Public APIs and protocols follow semantic versioning. Breaking changes require a major version bump, a migration path, and a reasonable deprecation window. Stability is a promise, not a suggestion.

## Rights of the Community

### 10. Right to Voice

Community members have the right to propose changes, raise concerns, and participate in governance discussions. Proposals must receive a substantive response within a documented timeframe.

### 11. Right to Accountability

The community has the right to hold Omni's stewards accountable to this constitution. Violations must be acknowledged publicly, with a clear remediation plan.

### 12. Right to Continuity

If Omni as an organization ceases to operate, all open-source projects must remain available under their existing licenses. No acquisition, dissolution, or restructuring may revoke rights previously granted.
